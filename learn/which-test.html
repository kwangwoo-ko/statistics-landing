Which statistical test should I use?

Three questions about your data — thirty seconds — and a recommendation with the reasoning spelled out.

Updated November 2026 · 30-second quiz · Plain English

Picking a test trips up almost everyone — and that is fine. The choice comes down to three properties of your data, not to how much statistics you remember. Answer below and you will see both the answer and the reason.

Question 1 of 3

How many groups (conditions) are you comparing?

How the decision works

The quiz implements the same decision table a statistician would sketch on a napkin:

Your dataRoughly normal, no strong outliersSkewed or has outliers
2 independent groups Welch's t-test
(Student's t-test if equal variance is a safe assumption)		 Mann-Whitney U test
3+ independent groups One-way ANOVA, then post-hoc comparisons Kruskal-Wallis test, then post-hoc comparisons
Paired measurements Paired t-test Wilcoxon signed-rank test

The one rule behind the table: parametric tests (t-test, ANOVA) compare means and assume the data is roughly bell-shaped. Rank-based tests (Mann-Whitney, Kruskal-Wallis) compare distributions and do not care about the shape. If a box or violin plot shows a strong skew or a lopsided tail, the rank-based test is the honest choice.

Two practical notes

Equal variance is rare in real data. That is why Welch's t-test is the default recommendation for two groups — it does not assume the two groups vary equally, and it is almost as powerful when they happen to. Student's t-test is fine when group sizes and spreads are similar.

"Not sure" about normality is a real answer. Plot your data (box or violin plot) before deciding. With 5–10 values per group, formal normality tests are unreliable anyway — a look at the shape is often the best evidence you have.

What to report with each test

  • t-test: the t statistic, degrees of freedom, the p-value, and the group means — with SD or SEM clearly labelled.
  • ANOVA: F, both degrees of freedom, p — and post-hoc results if the overall test is significant.
  • Mann-Whitney / Kruskal-Wallis: the U or H statistic, p, and group medians (or IQR) rather than means.
